Die Regeln

How a railway station earns points, how the table is sorted, and how it gets promoted or relegated.

1. Who plays

Every German station is a club. The only thing that counts is how punctually ICE and IC trains arrive there. Departures, regional trains, S-Bahn, EC/ECE and buses are ignored. A train's first stop has no arrival, so it does not count at that station.

A station plays on a given day only if it has more than 5 scheduled ICE/IC arrivals that day. With 5 or fewer, its matchday is abgesagt (called off): no points, and it does not count as a played game.

To keep it fair, only stations with a real ICE/IC service are in the league at all. To be a member for a week, a station must have had more than 5 scheduled ICE/IC arrivals on every single day of the 28 days before that week. A station that reaches 6 arrivals only on some days (weekends, holiday extras) is not a member, even on its busy days.

One exception protects against our data source, not against timetables: if the data for a whole day is clearly incomplete (fewer than 85% of the usual number of ICE/IC arrivals across all of Germany), that day is skipped for the membership check, so a collection outage can't throw anyone out.

2. Daily points: 0 to 1

Every day (one operating day, by planned arrival time) is a matchday. A station's points for the day are simply the share of its scheduled ICE/IC arrivals that were punctual:

Points = punctual arrivals ÷ scheduled arrivals, rounded to two decimals. So the range is 0.00 (nothing on time) to 1.00 (everything on time).

Punctual means the train arrived less than 6 minutes late, which is Deutsche Bahn's own threshold. A train 5 minutes late is punctual, 6 minutes late is not. A cancelled arrival counts as not punctual: it was scheduled, and passengers waiting for it did not get it.

Example (made-up numbers). Halle (Saale) Hbf has 40 scheduled ICE/IC arrivals on Tuesday. 29 arrive less than 6 minutes late, 8 arrive later, and 3 are cancelled. Points: 29 ÷ 40 = 0.73.

Köthen has 4 scheduled arrivals that day. That is not more than 5, so the matchday is abgesagt for Köthen.

3. The table

A season is one calendar week (KW), Monday to Sunday, so seven matchdays. The table is live: it is recalculated after every matchday, and the arrows show how many places a station moved since the previous day.

The columns are Platz, Bahnhof, Spiele (matchdays played this week), Punkte (sum of daily points), Schnitt (Punkte ÷ Spiele) and Form (the last five matchdays).

The table is sorted by Schnitt, not by the sum, so a station is not punished for a called-off day. Ties are broken by Punkte, then alphabetically. To be ranked normally a station must have played at least half of the matchdays so far (4 of 7 at the end of a week). Stations below that are listed at the bottom in grey, which in practice means they are in the relegation zone.

4. Promotion and relegation, every week

After Sunday's matchday the calendar week (KW) is final, and stations move between every pair of neighbouring leagues. Places 17 and 18 go down directly, and places 1 and 2 of the league below go up directly.

The third spot is decided in the Relegation: the 16th of the upper league plays the 3rd of the league below. There is no extra match. The winner is simply the station with the higher punctuality on the last day of that week (Sunday): the share of its ICE/IC arrivals under 6 minutes late, compared exactly, not rounded. The winner plays in the upper league next week, the loser in the lower one. Every playoff in the pyramid is decided this way.

Example (made-up numbers). Erfurt Hbf finishes 16th in the 1. Bundesbahnhofsliga, Fulda finishes 3rd in the 2. Bundesbahnhofsliga. On Sunday, 31 of 38 arrivals at Erfurt are punctual (81.6%) and 27 of 32 at Fulda (84.4%). Fulda goes up, Erfurt goes down.

If both are exactly level, the upper league's station stays up. A station whose Sunday is abgesagt (5 or fewer arrivals) cannot win the playoff; if both are abgesagt, the upper league's station stays up.

The new week starts on Monday with everyone back on zero. If a member drops out (it no longer meets the every-day rule), the next station from the league below moves up to fill the gap. New members start at the bottom of the pyramid.

5. The pyramid

Every league has 18 stations, like the Bundesliga: the 1. Bundesbahnhofsliga, the 2. Bundesbahnhofsliga, the 3. Bundesbahnhofsliga, and so on down. The lowest league takes whoever is left, so it can be smaller.

The very first season was seeded by the average daily points over the history available before it. Since then, the only way up is to play your way up.

6. Small print

Stations at the end of long routes inherit delays from upstream. That is fine for this league, because it measures what passengers at that station experience, but it means a low place is not a verdict on the station or the people working there. See the methodology page for the data details.
